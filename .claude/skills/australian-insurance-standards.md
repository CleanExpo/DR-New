## 1. Federal Regulatory Framework

### 1.1 Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Commonwealth)

**Legal Authority**: Federal legislation applying to all Australian insurance contracts.

**Key Principle - Utmost Good Faith (Section 13)**:
- Both insured party and insurer must act with utmost good faith
- Applies throughout the life of the policy
- Party with superior knowledge must disclose relevant facts
- Breach may render policy voidable
- Cannot be excluded by policy terms

**Practical Application**:
- Insurers must process claims fairly and transparently
- Claimants must provide accurate information when applying
- Neither party can act dishonestly or misleadingly
- Failure to disclose known material facts voids cover

**Reference**: [Federal Register of Legislation - Insurance Contracts Act 1984](https://www.legislation.gov.au/Latest/C2022C00015)

### 1.2 General Insurance Code of Practice 2020 (Updated October 2023)

**Governing Body**: Insurance Code Governance Committee (independent oversight)

**Mandatory Timeframes - All Insurers Must Comply**:

| Milestone | Timeline | Standard |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Claim Acknowledgment | 3 business days | Written acknowledgment required |
| Initial Response | 10 business days | Acknowledge receipt, request information |
| Progress Updates | Every 20 business days | Inform customer of claim status |
| Decision Notification | 4 months standard | Provide claim decision in writing |
| Decision Notification | 12 months exceptional | For complex/disputed claims only |
| Complaint Response | 30 days | Complete investigation and response |
| Expert Reports | 12 weeks maximum | Obtain and review required reports |

**Customer Service Obligations**:
- Respond to enquiries within 1-2 business days
- Provide plain English explanations of policy terms
- Assist with claim documentation requirements
- Advise on complaint/escalation processes
- Handle complaints professionally and promptly

**Premium Payment Protection**:
- Provide notice before cancellation for non-payment
- Allow reasonable time to remedy before cancellation
- Cannot cancel without written notice (except fraud)

**Claims Handling Standards**:
- Assess claims promptly based on available information
- Not unreasonably withhold or refuse claims
- Provide clear reasons for claim rejection
- Be transparent about policy exclusions
- Acknowledge special circumstances affecting customer (hardship, vulnerability)

**Reference**: [Insurance Code Governance Committee](https://insurancecode.org.au/) | [Code of Practice Full Text (PDF)](https://insurancecode.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/General-Insurance-Code-of-Practice-October-2023-Final.pdf)

### 1.3 AFCA - Australian Financial Complaints Authority

**Authority**: Independent dispute resolution scheme for Australian financial services consumers.

**Consumer Rights**:
- Free complaint service for eligible consumers
- Can escalate if insurer decision unsatisfactory
- Service available after 30 days of complaint lodge or if insurer deadline passed
- Can complain about claims handling, policy terms, sales practices

**AFCA Jurisdiction**:
- Covers claims up to **$631,500** (as of 2024)
- Can award compensation for financial loss and non-financial loss (inconvenience, distress)
- Binding on insurers; optional for consumers (consumers can pursue legal action if dissatisfied)

**AFCA Decision Timeline**:
- 21 calendar days to investigate
- Can extend to 43 days for complex cases
- Complainant notified of decision in writing
- Final and binding on insurer once issued

**When to Escalate to AFCA**:
- Insurer rejects claim without fair assessment
- Claim decision delayed beyond Code of Practice timeframes
- Dissatisfied with insurer's complaint response
- Believe insurer breached General Insurance Code of Practice
- Have suffered financial loss or inconvenience due to insurer conduct

**Reference**: [AFCA Official Website](https://www.afca.org.au/) | [AFCA Time Limits and Jurisdiction](https://www.afca.org.au/about-us/what-we-do)

## 2. Property Insurance Coverage by Type

### 2.1 Building Insurance

**Purpose**: Covers the structure of the building and permanent fixtures (walls, roof, built-in appliances, flooring, kitchen cupboards).

#### Standard Mandatory Inclusions (All Insurers)

✅ **Always Covered** (subject to policy excess):

- **Fire and Bushfire Damage**: Total or partial loss from fire
- **Storm and Storm Surge Damage**: Damage from wind, hail, torrential rain, lightning, storm surge
- **Tsunami Damage**: Water damage from tsunami events
- **Malicious Damage**: Vandalism, graffiti, deliberate property damage by third parties
- **Impact Damage**: Damage from vehicles, aircraft, falling objects
- **Escape of Liquid**: Water/liquid damage from burst pipes, leaking appliances, failed water heaters
- **Collapse**: Structural failure of building
- **Weight of Snow/Sleet**: Damage from accumulated snow (regional relevance)

#### Standard Exclusions (Universal - All Insurers)

❌ **Never Covered** (Standard policy wording):

- **Wear and Tear**: Gradual deterioration, normal aging, fading
- **Poor Maintenance**: Damage resulting from lack of upkeep or maintenance failure
- **Gradual Damage**: Slow water leaks from ongoing maintenance failures
- **Deliberate Acts by Policyholder**: Self-inflicted damage or intentional acts
- **Unlawful or Criminal Activity**: Damage caused by policyholder's illegal acts
- **War and Nuclear Events**: Damage from war, terrorism, nuclear incidents
- **Pre-Existing Damage**: Damage existing before policy commencement
- **Betterment**: Installing upgraded/superior materials at insurer's expense
- **Consequential Loss**: Business interruption, loss of rent (typically requires separate cover)

#### Optional Coverage - Building Insurance Add-ons

❌ **NOT Standard - Must Be Added** (Common optional coverage):

- **Flood Cover**: Water damage from rising flood waters
  - **Critical Note**: Flood is OPTIONAL for most major insurers (NRMA, Suncorp, QBE, IAG, CGU)
  - **Exception**: Allianz and AAMI include flood as standard with some policies
  - **Cost**: Typically 5-15% premium increase depending on location
  - **Why Optional**: Flood is difficult to predict, high-risk in certain areas

- **Accidental Damage**: Unintentional damage not covered by base policy
- **Enhanced Temporary Accommodation**: Higher caps for alternative housing during repairs
- **Fusion of Motors/Electrical**: Electrical damage to appliances and systems
- **Increased Sum Insured**: Above standard replacement value
- **Removal of Debris**: Coverage for professional debris removal (sometimes excluded)
- **Reinstatement Clause**: Replacement cost rather than indemnity value
- **Enhanced Underinsurance Protection**: Automatic protection if you're underinsured

### 2.2 Contents Insurance

**Purpose**: Covers personal belongings inside the building (furniture, appliances, clothing, electronics, goods owned by residents).

#### Standard Mandatory Inclusions

✅ **Always Covered** (subject to policy excess):

- **Fire and Bushfire Damage**: Destruction of contents by fire
- **Storm Damage**: Wind, hail, lightning damage to contents
- **Theft and Break-In**: Theft of items, damage from burglary
- **Malicious Damage**: Damage by third parties
- **Impact Damage**: Items damaged by falling objects, vehicles
- **Escape of Liquid**: Water damage to contents from burst pipes, leaking appliances
- **Temporary Accommodation**: Alternative housing costs during repairs (often capped)
  - Standard cap: $1,000-$3,000 per week
  - Duration: Typically 12-26 weeks maximum
  - Can be upgraded to higher limits
- **Removal and Replacement**: Coverage for essential items/temporary replacements

#### Standard Exclusions (Universal)

❌ **Never Covered**:

- **Wear and Tear**: Normal aging of possessions
- **Damage by Pets**: Destruction caused by resident animals
- **Gradual Damage**: Slow deterioration from moisture/humidity
- **Mechanical Breakdown**: Failure due to use and age
- **Consequential Loss**: Loss of use, business interruption
- **Pre-Existing Damage**: Damage before policy start

#### Optional Contents Coverage Add-ons

❌ **NOT Standard - Must Be Added**:

- **Accidental Damage**: Spills, breakage, unintended damage
- **Enhanced Temporary Accommodation**: Raise caps above standard limits
- **Removal of Debris**: Professional cleanup costs
- **Replacement Cost**: Replacement value rather than indemnity value
- **Items Away From Home**: Coverage for belongings temporarily away from property
- **Overseas Items**: Coverage for items while traveling
- **Vintage/Collectible Items**: Items with special value

### 2.3 Commercial Property Insurance

**Purpose**: Covers commercial/business properties and contents (office buildings, shops, factories, warehouses).

#### Standard Mandatory Inclusions

✅ **Always Covered**:

- **Fire and Bushfire**: Business premises and contents
- **Storm and Hail**: Damage to building and business property
- **Malicious Damage**: Vandalism to business premises
- **Impact Damage**: Vehicles, falling objects damaging business property
- **Escape of Liquid**: Burst pipes, water damage
- **Theft and Burglary**: Business goods and equipment

#### Standard Exclusions

❌ **Never Covered**:

- **Wear and Tear**
- **Poor Maintenance**
- **Gradual Damage**
- **War and Terrorism**
- **Cybercrime** (unless specific cyber policy)
- **Environmental Damage** (groundwater contamination, etc.)

#### Optional Commercial Coverage

❌ **NOT Standard**:

- **Business Interruption Insurance**: Loss of income during enforced closure
  - Covers: Lost profits, fixed costs (rent, salaries), rent payable
  - Standard period: 3-24 months depending on business type
  - **Critical**: Usually required by lenders/landlords

- **Flood Cover**: Water damage from flooding
  - Premium varies based on flood risk assessment

- **Cyber Insurance**: Breach, ransomware, business interruption from cyber attack
- **Equipment Breakdown**: Mechanical/electrical failure of machinery
- **Liability Extensions**: Enhanced coverage above standard limits
- **Stock Coverage**: Enhanced inventory protection
- **Emergency Access Costs**: Breaking in to property during emergency

### 2.4 Business Property Insurance (Inventory/Stock-Specific)

**Purpose**: Specialized coverage for business inventory, stock, and supplies.

#### Key Differences from Commercial Building Coverage

**Coverage Includes**:
- Stock held for sale
- Raw materials for manufacturing
- Work-in-progress items
- Finished goods ready for dispatch
- Supplies and consumables
- Tools and equipment specific to business

**Standard Protection**:
- Same perils as commercial property (fire, theft, storm)
- Indemnity value (actual cash value, not replacement cost)
- Inventory of items required for claim settlement
- Invoice records critical for proof of value

**Common Restrictions**:
- **Deterioration Exclusion**: Spoilage of perishables not covered
- **Underinsurance Clause**: Reduced payout if stock value underestimated
- **Maintenance Requirements**: Proper storage conditions required
- **Inventory Updates**: Regular stock count documentation required

## 3. Claims Process Standards

### 3.1 Timeline Requirements (Code of Practice Compliance)

**Day 3**: Claim Acknowledgment
- Written acknowledgment of claim receipt
- Provide claim reference number
- Outline next steps in process
- Provide contact details for questions

**Day 10**: Initial Response
- Acknowledge receipt of all documents submitted
- Identify any missing information needed
- Explain timeframe for claim decision
- Advise of customer's rights during investigation

**Every 20 Days**: Progress Updates
- Inform customer of investigation status
- Advise if additional information required
- Provide expected timeframe for decision
- Must continue even if awaiting customer response

**Day 30**: Complaint Response (if complaint made)
- Complete investigation of complaint
- Provide written response addressing all points
- Explain decision with full reasoning
- Advise of AFCA escalation rights

**4 Months**: Standard Claim Decision
- Provide final claim decision in writing
- If approved: Outline payment method and timing
- If rejected: Detailed reasons for rejection with reference to policy terms
- If partial: Explain reduction and reasoning

**12 Months**: Extended Timeline (Exceptional Cases Only)
- Complex claims requiring extensive investigation
- Disputes over coverage or valuation
- Third-party involvement (legal, expert assessment)
- Can only be used in genuinely complex circumstances

### 3.2 Documentation Requirements (Industry Standard)

**Essential Documentation for All Claims**:

1. **Proof of Loss/Incident**:
   - Written description of what happened (date, time, location)
   - Police report (if theft, vandalism, malicious damage)
   - Witness statements (if available)
   - Photos/video of damage

2. **Photographic Evidence**:
   - **Close-up photos**: Detail view of each damaged area
   - **Wide-angle photos**: Full context of room/space
   - **Multiple angles**: Show extent of damage from different positions
   - **Before and after**: If before-damage photos available (strengthens claim)
   - **Minimum requirement**: 2-3 high-quality photos per damage area
   - **Provider-specific**: NRMA requests 2+, Suncorp requests 3+, others vary

3. **Proof of Ownership/Value**:
   - Original receipts (ideal - shows purchase price and date)
   - Bank/credit card statements (showing purchase transaction)
   - Warranty cards/registration documents
   - Photos of items with identifying features
   - Item lists with descriptions
   - Serial numbers where available
   - Appraisals for high-value items

4. **Repair/Replacement Quotes**:
   - Multiple quotes recommended (minimum 2, ideally 3)
   - From reputable tradespersons/service providers
   - Itemized breakdown of costs
   - Scope of work clearly defined
   - Quote validity dates (typically 30 days)
   - Contact details of quoter

5. **Additional Evidence** (as applicable):
   - Repair invoices (if repairs already undertaken)
   - Receipts for emergency repairs (water extraction, temporary measures)
   - Tradesperson inspection reports
   - Structural engineer reports (major damage)
   - Mould/moisture assessment (water damage)
   - Council/building inspector reports

### 3.3 Consumer Rights During Claims Process

**Right to Information**:
- Clear explanation of policy coverage and exclusions
- Plain English explanations of technical terms
- Advance notice of any rejection or part-rejection
- Detailed reasons for any decision
- Information about complaint processes

**Right to Fair Assessment**:
- Insurer must assess claim based on available information
- Cannot unreasonably delay assessment
- Must consider all relevant evidence
- Cannot dismiss claim without investigation
- Must follow Code of Practice timeframes

**Cooling-Off Period** (Purchase/Renewal):
- **Timeline**: 14 calendar days from policy commencement
- **Duration**: Extends to 21 days if policy documents received late
- **Process**: Written notice required to cancel
- **Refund**: Full refund of premium if no claim made during period
- **Exclusions**: Doesn't apply if claim already made
- **Important**: Applies to new policies and renewals

**Right to Complaint**:
- Can lodge complaint with insurer at any time
- Insurer must acknowledge within 1 business day
- Insurer must respond within 30 days (Code of Practice requirement)
- Can escalate to AFCA after 30 days or if dissatisfied with response

**Right to AFCA Escalation**:
- Available if complaint not resolved to satisfaction
- No time limit to escalate (can escalate immediately or after 30 days)
- Free service for eligible consumers
- Can claim up to $631,500
- Decision binding on insurer

## 4. Contractor Insurance Requirements

### 4.1 Public Liability Insurance

**Purpose**: Covers contractor's legal liability for bodily injury or property damage caused to third parties during contract work.

**Statutory Requirements**:
- **Federal**: No federal legal minimum (not mandated by law)
- **Contractual**: Required by contracts with clients, insurers, large organizations
- **Market Standard**: Set by contractual requirements, not legislation
- **Verification**: Certificate of Currency required by most clients

**Industry Standards by Work Type**:

| Work Type | Minimum Cover | Typical Cover | Comments |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|----------|
| Small residential | $5M | $10M | Disaster/restoration work exceeds typical needs |
| Water damage/restoration | $10M | $10-20M | **Industry standard for disaster work** |
| Mould remediation | $5-10M | $10M | Specialized risk requiring higher cover |
| Fire/smoke restoration | $10M | $10-20M | Large-scale damage potential |
| Large commercial | $10-20M | $20M | Multi-site, high-value loss potential |
| Asbestos removal | $20M | $20-50M | Extremely high liability risk |

**Critical Note for Disaster Recovery Work**: Contractors working on insurance-approved restoration typically require **minimum $10M public liability insurance**. This is not a legal requirement but a practical industry standard set by insurers and clients.

**Certificate of Currency**:
- Document proving active public liability insurance
- Should show:
  - Contractor business name
  - Policy number and expiry date
  - Coverage limit ($10M for disaster work)
  - Insurer details
  - Effective date
- **Validity**: Typically valid for policy year (usually 12 months)
- **Requirement**: Clients/insurers often request before work commences

**Coverage Scope**:
- Personal injury (bodily harm to third party)
- Property damage (damage to third party's property)
- Includes legal costs of defense
- Covers events during contract period

**Typical Exclusions**:
- Contractor's own property damage
- Employee injuries (covered under workers compensation)
- Contractual liability (some policies exclude unless endorsed)
- Professional liability (separate policy required)

### 4.2 Workers Compensation Insurance (WorkCover)

**Statutory Requirement**: Mandatory for all employees; state-specific requirements apply.

**Federal Framework**:
- No single federal WorkCover scheme
- Managed by state-based authorities (WorkSafe/WorkCover corporations)
- Each state has different rates, requirements, and coverage details

**State Authorities**:
- **New South Wales**: WorkCover NSW
- **Victoria**: WorkSafe Victoria
- **Queensland**: WorkSafe Queensland
- **South Australia**: ReturnToWorkSA
- **Western Australia**: WorkSafe WA
- **Tasmania**: WorkSafe Tasmania
- **ACT**: ACT Workcover
- **NT**: NT Workcover

**Mandatory Coverage - Who Must Be Covered**:
- Employees (even casual, part-time, labor-hire)
- Can include family members in family businesses (varies by state)
- **Contractors with ABN**: May be deemed "workers" if relationship suggests employment

**Typical Coverage Includes**:
- Medical and hospital treatment (full cost)
- Weekly compensation (income support during recovery)
- Return-to-work support and rehabilitation
- Lump sum for permanent disability
- Death benefits to dependents
- Funeral expenses

**Common Exclusions**:
- Contractors (unless specific agreement treats them as workers)
- Volunteers (in some states)
- Self-employed sole traders (state-dependent)
- Family members not registered (state-dependent)

**Important for Disaster Recovery**: Contractors should verify their workers compensation obligations in their state, as this affects insurance costs and client compliance requirements.

### 4.3 Professional Indemnity Insurance

**Current Status**: Varies by state and profession; major regulatory change coming.

**New South Wales - Major Regulatory Change (July 2026)**:

**Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020**:
- **Effective Date**: 1 July 2026
- **Mandatory For**: Design and Building Practitioners in NSW
- **Coverage Required**: Professional Indemnity Insurance
- **Minimum Amount**: Varies by contract value
  - Contracts < $20M: $1M minimum
  - Contracts $20-100M: $2M minimum
  - Contracts > $100M: $5M minimum

- **Why This Matters**: Restoration contractors may be caught by this if they perform design or building work (not just physical restoration)

**Current Status (Other States)**:
- **Not yet mandatory** in other states (as of 2026)
- **Strongly recommended**: For contractors doing design/specification work
- **Increasingly required**: By client contracts and insurer agreements
- **Cost**: Typically $1,000-$5,000 annually depending on coverage level

**What Professional Indemnity Covers**:
- Negligent advice or design causing financial loss
- Professional errors in specification or scope definition
- Failure to meet professional standards
- Includes legal defense costs

**10-Year Duty of Care** (NSW):
- Building Work clause creates statutory duty of care
- Contractors can be held liable for defects discovered up to 10 years after work
- Encouraged to carry long-tail professional indemnity cover
- Important consideration for disaster recovery work where long-term issues may emerge

### 4.4 Insurance Verification Process (For Disaster Recovery Platforms)

**Before Contractor Approval**:

1. **Public Liability Insurance**:
   - Request Certificate of Currency
   - Verify minimum $10M coverage for disaster work
   - Check policy expiry date (must be current)
   - Confirm contractor business name matches certificate
   - Verify insurer details

2. **Workers Compensation Insurance**:
   - Confirm registration with state WorkCover authority
   - Verify coverage includes all employees
   - Request proof of current compliance
   - Note state-specific obligations met

3. **Professional Indemnity Insurance** (if applicable):
   - Request certificate if contractor does design/specification work
   - Confirm coverage amounts match contract values
   - Verify current and valid

**Annual Re-verification**:
- Request updated certificates before policy expiry
- Maintain insurance compliance records
- Flag to contractor if compliance lapsing
- Consider deactivating contractor if insurance lapses

## 5. Policy Exclusions and Common Misconceptions

### 5.1 Universal Exclusions (All Insurers)

**Standard Exclusions Found in All Property Insurance Policies**:

| Exclusion | Why Excluded | Impact |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Wear and Tear** | Normal aging is maintenance responsibility | Regular fading, minor deterioration not covered |
| **Gradual Deterioration** | Slow changes due to climate/use | Slow water damage, rust, corrosion not covered |
| **Poor Maintenance** | Property owner responsibility | Damage from lack of upkeep not covered |
| **Deliberate Acts (Policyholder)** | Fraud prevention | Intentional damage by owner not covered |
| **Unlawful/Criminal Acts** | Fraud prevention | Damage from illegal activity not covered |
| **Pre-Existing Damage** | Must exist before policy start | Damage visible before commencement not covered |
| **Betterment** | Insurer won't upgrade | Replacement must match original standards |
| **War/Terrorism/Nuclear** | Uninsurable risks | Damage from war, terrorism, nuclear not covered |

### 5.2 Common Misconceptions

**Misconception 1: "Flood is always covered in building insurance"**
- **Reality**: Flood is OPTIONAL for most major Australian insurers
- **Standard in**: Allianz (most policies), AAMI (select policies)
- **Optional in**: NRMA, Suncorp, QBE, IAG, CGU, Medibank
- **Cost**: Typically adds 5-15% to premium depending on location
- **Lesson for Clients**: Always check PDS (Product Disclosure Statement) for flood exclusions

**Misconception 2: "Insurance covers all water damage"**
- **Reality**: Specific water events covered, others excluded
- **Covered**: Burst pipes, storm surge, escape of liquid from appliances
- **Not Covered**: Flood (usually optional), seepage, rising groundwater, condensation
- **Distinction Matters**: Water source determines coverage eligibility

**Misconception 3: "If I upgrade my home, insurance covers full replacement cost"**
- **Reality**: Betterment exclusions apply
- **Betterment Definition**: Installing superior/upgraded materials at insurer expense
- **Example**: If roof was 20 years old (should be 25), new roof installed at standard cost, not premium cost
- **Implication**: Upgrades may be customer's responsibility, not insurer's

**Misconception 4: "I can claim for items I lost but can't find receipts for"**
- **Reality**: Proof of ownership required
- **Acceptable Evidence**: Bank statements, credit card records, photos, warranty cards, supplier records
- **Challenge**: Items without proof difficult to value
- **Lesson**: Keep receipts, take inventory photos with items visible

**Misconception 5: "My claim will be approved within 10 days"**
- **Reality**: 10 days is for initial response only
- **Timeline**: 3 days acknowledgment, 10 days initial response, 4 months to decision
- **Reality**: Complex claims take full 4 months or up to 12 months for exceptional cases
- **Lesson**: Plan for extended timeframe; expedited claims uncommon

**Misconception 6: "Contractors must carry $20M public liability insurance"**
- **Reality**: Standard is $10M for most disaster work
- **Why $10M**: Covers typical large-scale restoration projects
- **When Higher**: Asbestos removal, large commercial projects may require $20-50M
- **Not Legal**: No federal requirement; contractually determined

### 5.3 Policy Exclusion Case Examples

**Case 1: Water Damage Classification**
- **Scenario**: Heavy rain causes water to seep through wall cracks (gradual)
- **Covered**: ❌ Not covered (gradual deterioration)
- **Reason**: Water damage from poor maintenance/lack of sealant
- **Lesson**: Seepage from maintenance failure is excluded

- **Scenario**: Burst pipe from freezing damage (sudden event)
- **Covered**: ✅ Covered (escape of liquid)
- **Reason**: Sudden unexpected damage from burst pipe
- **Lesson**: Sudden water incidents usually covered

**Case 2: Flood vs Storm Water**
- **Scenario**: Heavy storm + rising creek causes water entry through door (simultaneous)
- **Covered**: ❌ May be declined if primary cause is flood
- **Reason**: Flood exclusion applies unless flood cover added
- **Lesson**: Source of water (rain vs flood) determines coverage

- **Scenario**: Heavy storm causes gutter failure, water leaks through ceiling
- **Covered**: ✅ Covered (storm damage)
- **Reason**: Storm caused sudden damage
- **Lesson**: Damage from weather events typically covered

**Case 3: Maintenance and Wear**
- **Scenario**: Roof 25 years old develops leak (normal lifespan expiration)
- **Covered**: ❌ Not covered (wear and tear)
- **Reason**: Roof reached end of typical service life
- **Lesson**: Maintenance prevention is owner responsibility

- **Scenario**: Roof 10 years old blown off by severe storm
- **Covered**: ✅ Covered (storm damage)
- **Reason**: Storm caused sudden unexpected damage
- **Lesson**: Age irrelevant if sudden weather event

## 6. Claim Documentation Checklist (Best Practices)

### Essential Documents for Stronger Claims:

**Timing**:
- [ ] Document damage immediately (photos same day)
- [ ] Get repair quotes within 5 days
- [ ] Submit claim within 14 days of incident
- [ ] Gather supporting documents within 7 days

**Photographic Evidence**:
- [ ] Close-up photos of each damage area (2-3 per area)
- [ ] Wide-angle photos showing full context
- [ ] Photos from multiple angles showing extent
- [ ] Date stamp on all photos (phone camera recommended)
- [ ] Before damage photos if available from prior inspections

**Incident Documentation**:
- [ ] Written description: Date, time, location, what happened
- [ ] Weather information: Rain gauge readings, weather bureau data
- [ ] Police report (if theft/vandalism)
- [ ] Witness statements and contact details
- [ ] Council/building inspection reports

**Ownership Proof**:
- [ ] Original purchase receipts (where available)
- [ ] Bank/credit card statements showing purchase
- [ ] Warranty cards and registration documents
- [ ] Serial numbers of damaged items
- [ ] Appraisals for high-value items
- [ ] Photos of items pre-damage

**Repair Information**:
- [ ] Quotes from 2-3 qualified tradespersons
- [ ] Itemized breakdown of costs
- [ ] Scope of work definition
- [ ] Tradesperson qualifications and license details
- [ ] Completed invoices (if repairs already done)

**Professional Reports** (for significant damage):
- [ ] Building/structural engineer assessment
- [ ] Moisture/mould specialist report (water damage)
- [ ] Restoration specialist report (IICRC-certified preferred)
- [ ] Product testing results (if contamination suspected)

## 7. Key Terminology Reference

**Indemnity Value**: The actual cash value of property accounting for age and condition (less favorable to claimant than replacement cost).

**Replacement Cost**: The cost to replace property with new equivalent (more favorable than indemnity value).

**Sum Insured**: The maximum amount insurer will pay for a claim; it's the limit of coverage.

**Excess**: The amount claimant must pay toward claim cost; insurer pays amount above excess up to sum insured.

**Claim Ratio**: Number of claims compared to premiums paid (high ratio = insurer may decline renewal).

**Duty of Disclosure**: Requirement to disclose all material information when applying for insurance.

**Betterment**: Installing superior/upgraded materials; typically not covered by insurance (policyholder pays difference).

**Deeming**: When a contractor with ABN is treated as an employee for WorkCover purposes.

**Certificate of Currency**: Document proving current active insurance coverage.

**PDS (Product Disclosure Statement)**: Mandatory document explaining policy coverage, exclusions, terms.

**AFCA**: Australian Financial Complaints Authority - independent dispute resolution service.

**Code of Practice**: General Insurance Code of Practice 2020 - binding timeframes and service standards.

## 8. Quick Reference - Insurance by Scenario

### Residential Property Damage Scenarios:

**Pipe Burst (Winter)**:
- Building Insurance: ✅ Covered (escape of liquid)
- Contents Insurance: ✅ Covered (water damage to contents)
- Timeline: 3-10 day initial response expected

**Bushfire Damage**:
- Building Insurance: ✅ Covered (fire damage)
- Contents Insurance: ✅ Covered (fire damage)
- Timeline: 10-20 days initial response (high volume of claims)
- Note: May face insurer capacity constraints

**Flooding (No Flood Cover)**:
- Building Insurance: ❌ Not covered (flood exclusion)
- Contents Insurance: ❌ Not covered (flood exclusion)
- Recommendation: Add flood cover before next renewal
- Timeline: N/A - claim ineligible

**Storm Damage**:
- Building Insurance: ✅ Covered (storm damage)
- Contents Insurance: ✅ Covered (storm damage)
- Timeline: 5-10 day initial response expected

**Theft/Burglary**:
- Building Insurance: ✅ Covered (malicious damage from break-in)
- Contents Insurance: ✅ Covered (theft of items)
- Required: Police report for both
- Timeline: 10 day initial response standard

**Mould After Water Damage**:
- Building Insurance: ✅ Covered if caused by covered incident (pipe burst, flood water)
- Contents Insurance: ✅ Covered if caused by covered incident
- Requirements: Moisture specialist report recommended
- Note: Mould from ongoing moisture = not covered (maintenance)
