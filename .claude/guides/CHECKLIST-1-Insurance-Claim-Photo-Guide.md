# Insurance Claim Photo Documentation Checklist

> Downloadable: Print this checklist before documenting damage
> Used by: Guide 1 & Guide 7
> Status: Ready to PDF
> Format: Printable checklist for on-site documentation

---

## Before You Start Photography

### Preparation Checklist
- [ ] Charge phone battery fully (or bring charging cable)
- [ ] Ensure good lighting (take photos in daylight if possible)
- [ ] Wear protective gear if area is hazardous
- [ ] Clear area of obstacles for clear photos
- [ ] Have something for scale reference (ruler, coin, person)
- [ ] Note current date/time before starting

### Camera Settings
- [ ] Use phone camera (date stamp automatic)
- [ ] Enable location services (geo-tagging helps)
- [ ] Use high resolution setting (not low quality)
- [ ] Clean camera lens before starting
- [ ] Disable flash for outdoor photos (causes glare)

## Damage Documentation - By Location

### Exterior Damage Photos

#### Roof Damage
- [ ] Full roof overview (from ground level)
- [ ] Close-up of damaged areas (3+ angles)
- [ ] Close-up of intact roof (for comparison)
- [ ] Missing tiles/sections (detail views)
- [ ] Gutters and downpipes (damage areas)
- [ ] Flashing damage (if present)
- [ ] Water entry points (if visible)

#### Wall/Facade Damage
- [ ] Full wall overview
- [ ] Close-up of cracks (multiple angles)
- [ ] Holes or impact damage (3+ views)
- [ ] Paint damage/staining
- [ ] Water damage patterns (if present)
- [ ] Window/door frame damage
- [ ] Siding/cladding damage

#### Foundation/Ground Level
- [ ] Soil erosion (if applicable)
- [ ] Foundation cracks
- [ ] Flooding marks on walls
- [ ] Water staining (height of water line)
- [ ] Debris accumulation

#### Fencing/Gates
- [ ] Fence overview
- [ ] Damaged sections (detail)
- [ ] Posts or supports damage
- [ ] Gate damage (if applicable)
- [ ] Entire fence perimeter (if comprehensive)

### Interior Damage Photos

#### Each Affected Room - Photograph EVERYTHING

**Standard Room Documentation**:
- [ ] Full room overview (wide angle, from doorway)
- [ ] Each wall (close-up of damage areas)
- [ ] Ceiling damage (if present)
- [ ] Floor damage (wet, stained, buckled)
- [ ] Windows and window frames (damage)
- [ ] Doors and door frames (damage)
- [ ] Built-in elements (cupboards, cabinets damage)

**Water Damage Specific**:
- [ ] Waterline height on walls (measure and note)
- [ ] Staining patterns
- [ ] Wet areas still saturated
- [ ] Damage to baseboards/skirting
- [ ] Moisture visible in corners/joins
- [ ] Swelling or deformation of materials
- [ ] Mold beginning to appear (if visible)

**Fire/Smoke Damage Specific**:
- [ ] Char marks and burn patterns
- [ ] Smoke staining on walls/ceilings
- [ ] Soot deposits
- [ ] Damage to contents visible through damage
- [ ] Heat damage (melted items, warped materials)
- [ ] Odor evidence (photo showing extent of damage)

**Structural Damage Specific**:
- [ ] Cracks in walls (measure length/width, note location)
- [ ] Ceiling collapse areas
- [ ] Floor damage (buckled, soft spots, holes)
- [ ] Structural supports damaged
- [ ] Walls leaning or misaligned

#### Kitchen (If Damaged)
- [ ] Appliances damaged (each one: close-up)
- [ ] Cabinets damage (doors, hinges, structure)
- [ ] Bench top/counters damage
- [ ] Backsplash damage
- [ ] Flooring damage
- [ ] Walls and ceilings

#### Bathroom (If Damaged)
- [ ] Vanity damage
- [ ] Toilet/bidet damage
- [ ] Shower/bathtub damage
- [ ] Walls and fixtures
- [ ] Flooring damage
- [ ] Mirror/medicine cabinet damage

#### Bedrooms (If Damaged)
- [ ] Damage to walls/ceilings
- [ ] Built-in wardrobes damage
- [ ] Window damage
- [ ] Flooring damage
- [ ] Any other fixtures affected

#### Laundry/Utility Areas
- [ ] Water heater (if damaged)
- [ ] Washing machine (if damaged)
- [ ] Electrical box/panel (if damaged by water)
- [ ] Pipes or plumbing visible damage
- [ ] Flooring damage

### Contents Damage Photos

#### Damaged Furniture

#### Before/During/After Sequence

## Contents Documentation - Parallel to Photos

### Item-by-Item List (Create Separate Spreadsheet or Document)

For each damaged item photograph, also document:
- [ ] Item description (model, color, material)
- [ ] Approximate age
- [ ] Original purchase price (if known)
- [ ] Current estimated replacement cost
- [ ] Photo filename reference
- [ ] Condition before damage
- [ ] Damage description
- [ ] Repair possible? (Yes/No/Unknown)

### Example Entry Format:
```
Item: BREVILLE COFFEE MAKER (MODEL XXXX)
Color: Stainless steel
Purchase Date: 2020
Original Price: $299
Replacement Cost: $250 (current model)
Photo References: IMG_001.jpg, IMG_002.jpg
Damage: Water-logged interior, corroded electrical components
Repair: No - recommend replacement
```

## Special Documentation Requirements

### Water Damage Claims
- [ ] Water entry point documented (how water entered)
- [ ] Water level height photographed (with measurement scale)
- [ ] Moisture meter reading (if you have one)
- [ ] Timeline photo (damage progression if visible)
- [ ] Surrounding undamaged areas (comparison)
- [ ] Drainage/water escape route shown

### Fire/Smoke Damage Claims
- [ ] Fire origin area (if safe to document)
- [ ] Burn patterns showing fire spread
- [ ] Heat damage progression
- [ ] Smoke staining extent
- [ ] Window/door openings showing fire exposure
- [ ] Charred materials (detail view)
- [ ] Unburnt/protected areas (for comparison)

### Storm Damage Claims
- [ ] Tree damage (fallen branches, whole trees)
- [ ] Impact points (where objects hit)
- [ ] Weather evidence if possible (standing water, debris scatter pattern)
- [ ] Roof damage from weather (slate/tile loss)
- [ ] Structural damage from impact
- [ ] Surrounding undamaged properties (shows storm severity)

### Theft/Burglary Claims
- [ ] Forced entry point (door/window/wall)
- [ ] Lock damage
- [ ] Items removed (photo empty spaces)
- [ ] Items left behind (shows selectivity of theft)
- [ ] Police report reference number (document taken)
- [ ] Police report photos (if available)

### Malicious Damage Claims
- [ ] Graffiti/vandalism full documentation
- [ ] Close-up detail of damage
- [ ] Extent of damage (full overview)
- [ ] Any message/pattern indicating intent
- [ ] Police report evidence (if reported)
- [ ] Witness contacts (if any observed)

## Photography Best Practices

### Lighting
- [ ] Photograph in daylight when possible
- [ ] Use natural light (north-facing windows, cloudy days)
- [ ] Avoid backlighting (photographer's shadow in photo)
- [ ] For interior: open curtains/blinds for natural light
- [ ] Use flash for dark areas (but avoid shiny surface glare)
- [ ] Take multiple shots (lighting changes between photos)

### Angles & Framing
- [ ] Take photos straight-on when possible (not angled)
- [ ] Include context (surrounding undamaged areas)
- [ ] Close-ups show damage detail
- [ ] Wide shots show overall extent
- [ ] Multiple angles for large areas (different viewpoints)
- [ ] Vertical AND horizontal orientations (adjust to fit subject)

### Composition
- [ ] Include scale reference when possible (ruler, coin, person)
- [ ] Keep objects in focus (avoid blurry movement)
- [ ] Remove obstructions (move items blocking view if safe)
- [ ] Timestamp all photos (phone default usually works)
- [ ] Photo filenames: DATE_LOCATION_DESCRIPTION (e.g., 2026-01-09_KITCHEN_WATER_DAMAGE)

## Photo Organization & Storage

### Immediate Storage
- [ ] Copy all photos to computer immediately (backup)
- [ ] Create folder structure: Claim_Date > Room > Photo Type
- [ ] Name files systematically (date_location_description)
- [ ] Store in cloud backup (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive)
- [ ] Keep originals (full resolution, not compressed)

### Documentation Preparation
- [ ] Create spreadsheet: Photo filename, Location, Item, Value, Damage Type
- [ ] Cross-reference photos to contents list
- [ ] Organize by room/area for insurer clarity
- [ ] Print key photos (insurer may request physical prints)
- [ ] Prepare USB drive with all photos (for insurer submission)

### Submission to Insurer
- [ ] Ask insurer preferred format (email, USB, online portal)
- [ ] Confirm all photos submitted (get confirmation)
- [ ] Keep records of submission date/method
- [ ] Request receipt acknowledgment from insurer
- [ ] Maintain copies for your records

## Timing Notes

### Photograph As Soon As Possible
- [ ] Photograph within 24 hours of loss (fresh condition)
- [ ] Water damage worse if left wet - document immediately
- [ ] Don't delay for insurer approval - document independently
- [ ] If mold developing - document before and after remediation
- [ ] Changes in damage (water receding, mold spreading) - re-photograph

### Hazards to Consider
- [ ] Wait for safety clearance if structural damage severe
- [ ] Avoid wet electrical areas (electrocution risk)
- [ ] Don't walk on damaged floors (collapse risk)
- [ ] Use protective equipment if required (boots, gloves, mask)
- [ ] Document hazards in photos (for context)

## When Submitting to Insurance

### What to Submit
- [ ] Original full-resolution photos (not compressed)
- [ ] Photo index document (spreadsheet listing all photos)
- [ ] Item list with replacement values
- [ ] Quotes for repair/replacement (professional estimates)
- [ ] Proof of ownership for items (receipts, warranties)
- [ ] Written claim description (what, when, how)

### What NOT to Submit
- [ ] Don't submit blurry/poor quality duplicates
- [ ] Don't manipulate or edit photos (send originals)
- [ ] Don't withhold photos showing undamaged areas (context needed)
- [ ] Don't include family members unless relevant to damage

### Follow-Up
- [ ] Confirm insurer received all photos
- [ ] Request acknowledgment of receipt
- [ ] Keep records of submission dates
- [ ] Be prepared to take additional photos if insurer requests
- [ ] Don't discard original photos (may need them for dispute)

## Troubleshooting Common Photo Issues

### "Photos are too blurry"
- **Cause**: Phone movement, low light, dust on lens
- **Solution**: Clean lens, use better lighting, hold camera steady (use both hands)

### "Can't see the damage clearly"
- **Cause**: Wrong distance, poor angle, insufficient lighting
- **Solution**: Take multiple angles, get closer for detail, use better lighting

### "Photos don't show the extent of damage"
- **Cause**: Only close-ups, missing context photos
- **Solution**: Take wide overview photos PLUS close-ups for each area

### "Color doesn't match what I saw"
- **Cause**: Lighting or phone white balance
- **Solution**: Take multiple shots in different lighting, white balance may vary

### "Photos don't have date stamps"
- **Cause**: Phone settings disabled, camera doesn't timestamp
- **Solution**: Enable date stamp in phone camera settings before photographing

## Questions to Answer While Photographing

As you document, also note answers to these questions:

### Damage Discovery & Timeline
- [ ] When was damage first discovered? (Date/time)
- [ ] How was damage discovered? (Visual inspection, water dripping, etc.)
- [ ] What was the trigger/cause? (Storm, pipe burst, intrusion, etc.)
- [ ] How long before reporting to insurer?

### Damage Extent & Progression
- [ ] Is damage still occurring? (Ongoing leak, active fire, etc.)
- [ ] Have repairs started? (No - submit photos before repairs)
- [ ] Is damage spreading? (Mold, water creeping, etc.)
- [ ] What temporary measures taken? (Tarps, buckets, boarding up, etc.)

### Affected Items & Values
- [ ] List all damaged items with approximate replacement costs
- [ ] Separate contents into categories (furniture, electronics, etc.)
- [ ] Estimate percentage of each category damaged (10%, 50%, 100%)
- [ ] Any high-value items affected? (Jewelry, art, antiques, etc.)

### Access & Mitigation
- [ ] Are items accessible for photography? (Safety concern?)
- [ ] Have any items been removed for drying/cleaning?
- [ ] Can repairs begin or must wait for insurer inspection?
- [ ] Any temporary expenses incurred? (Emergency repairs, accommodation)
