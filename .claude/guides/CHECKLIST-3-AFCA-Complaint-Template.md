## BEFORE YOU START - Know When to Use AFCA

### When AFCA Complaint is Appropriate
- [ ] You've lodged complaint with insurer (via email/letter)
- [ ] Insurer provided response but you disagree
- [ ] It's been 30 days since you complained to insurer (no response)
- [ ] Insurer's response unfair or breaches Code of Practice
- [ ] You believe insurer acted unreasonably or negligently

### When AFCA Complaint is NOT Appropriate
- [ ] You haven't complained to insurer first
- [ ] Claim is within 30-day insurer response period (wait first)
- [ ] Issue is criminal (report to police instead)
- [ ] Issue involves personal injury (sue in court instead)
- [ ] AFCA timeframe expired (3 years from issue arising)

## SECTION 10: AFCA Escalation Checklist

### Before Submitting to AFCA - Confirm:

- [ ] I have lodged formal written complaint with insurer
- [ ] More than 30 days have passed since insurer's complaint response
  - OR insurer failed to respond within 30 days of complaint
- [ ] I'm not satisfied with insurer's response
- [ ] I haven't already escalated this complaint to AFCA
- [ ] The issue occurred within the last 3 years
- [ ] AFCA has jurisdiction (financial services consumer complaint)
- [ ] I'm providing accurate and truthful information
- [ ] I've gathered all supporting evidence
- [ ] I've clearly stated what resolution I'm seeking

### Jurisdiction Confirmation

AFCA can only handle complaints involving:
- [ ] Financial services provided by a service provider

## SECTION 14: Following Up on AFCA Complaint

### What to Do While AFCA Investigates

**Do**:
- [ ] Respond promptly to any AFCA requests for information
- [ ] Keep all communication records (emails, letters)
- [ ] Monitor email for AFCA updates
- [ ] Respond to insurer's response (if they submit one)
- [ ] Note any new information that strengthens your case

**Don't**:
- [ ] Contact insurer again during AFCA investigation
- [ ] Accept settlement from insurer (voids AFCA process)
- [ ] Provide different information to AFCA than to insurer
- [ ] Lose patience - AFCA process takes 4-6 weeks typically
- [ ] Make assumptions about AFCA decision until received

### What If You Disagree with AFCA Decision?

```
If you disagree with AFCA determination:

Options:
☐ Accept AFCA decision (insurer must pay)
☐ Pursue court action (legal claim against insurer)
☐ Accept partial decision and pursue court for difference
☐ Request AFCA reconsideration (limited circumstances)

Legal advice recommended if disputing decision.
Consider: solicitor consultation, legal costs, likelihood of success.

Timeline: Can pursue court action after receiving AFCA decision.
Cost: Legal fees apply (no longer free like AFCA process).
```

## Important Notes

**AFCA Processing**:
- Takes 4-6 weeks typically (can be longer for complex cases)
- Free service (no cost to you)
- Binding on insurer (they must accept determination)
- Optional for you (can still pursue court action)

**What AFCA Cannot Do**:
- Award more than $631,500 (as of 2024)
- Punish insurer beyond award to you
- Award punitive damages
- Force settlement if case unmeritorious
- Handle complaints outside 3-year timeframe

**Your Rights**:
- Right to free representation (legal advice)
- Right to full investigation
- Right to response to insurer's arguments
- Right to written determination with reasons
- Right to appeal/pursue court action if disagree

---

**Format**: Can be printed and filled by hand, or completed digitally
