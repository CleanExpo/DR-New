## Quick Decision Tool - Use This First

### Should I Make an Insurance Claim?

Before calculating, answer these questions:

**Question 1: Is damage significant?**
```
Estimate damage cost: $__________

If less than $500 → Skip claim (excess usually higher)
If $500-$2,000 → Use calculator below
If more than $2,000 → Almost certainly claim
```

**Question 2: Is this your first claim in 3+ years?**
```
Last claim date: _________________________
Months since last claim: __________

If 3+ years since last claim → Premium impact lower (claim more likely worth it)
If less than 3 years → Premium impact higher (claim less likely worth it)
If this is your first claim → Premium impact is standard (claim likely worth it)
```

**Question 3: Was damage your fault or environmental?**
```
Damage cause: ☐ Storm  ☐ Burst pipe  ☐ Malicious damage  ☐ Theft  ☐ My negligence  ☐ Other

Not your fault (natural event) → Lower premium impact (claim more likely worth it)
Your fault (negligence) → Higher premium impact (claim less likely worth it)
```

## SECTION 4: Scenario Planning

### Scenario A: Water Damage (Typical)

```
SCENARIO A: BURST PIPE - WATER DAMAGE
═════════════════════════════════════

Damage Details:
- Kitchen flooded
- Flooring, cabinetry, appliances affected
- Total damage: $3,500

Your Details:
- Annual premium: $1,200
- Excess: $1,000
- First claim in 10 years

Calculation:
Damage: $3,500
Minus excess: -$1,000
Insurer pays: $2,500

Premium increase (15% typical): $180/year
5-year impact: $180 × 2.5 = $450
Total cost of claiming: $1,000 + $450 = $1,450

Result: Save $3,500 - $1,450 = $2,050 by claiming ✓

DECISION: CLAIM (Save $2,050)
```

### Scenario B: Small Accident (Questionable)

```
SCENARIO B: BROKEN WINDOW - STORM DAMAGE
═════════════════════════════════════════

Damage Details:
- Window broken in storm
- Replacement cost: $800

Your Details:
- Annual premium: $900
- Excess: $750
- Second claim in 2 years

Calculation:
Damage: $800
Minus excess: -$750
Insurer pays: $50

Premium increase (25% typical for second claim): $225/year
5-year impact: $225 × 2.5 = $562.50
Total cost of claiming: $750 + $562.50 = $1,312.50

Result: LOSE $800 vs $1,312.50 cost = LOSE $512.50 by claiming ✗

DECISION: DON'T CLAIM (Save $512 by paying yourself)
```

## SECTION 5: Premium Impact Analysis

### Understanding How Claims Affect Your Premium

```
CLAIMS HISTORY IMPACT TABLE
════════════════════════════

Years Since Claim    Impact on Premium    Notes
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
0 years (current)    Full impact (100%)   Year claim made
1 year later         90% of original      Still significant
2 years later        70% of original      Reducing slowly
3 years later        50% of original      Halfway reduced
4 years later        20% of original      Almost normal
5+ years later       0% (removed)         No longer considered

Example with $200 annual impact:
Year 1: +$200 (full impact)
Year 2: +$180 (90%)
Year 3: +$140 (70%)
Year 4: +$100 (50%)
Year 5: +$40 (20%)
Total 5-year cost: $660
```

### How Multiple Claims Affect Premium

```
MULTIPLE CLAIMS - PREMIUM STACKING
═══════════════════════════════════

Scenario: 2 claims within 2 years

Claim 1 (Year 1): +20% premium increase
Year 1: $1,000 × 1.20 = $1,200
Year 2: $1,000 × 1.18 = $1,180 (original + combined impact)
Year 3: $1,000 × 1.14 = $1,140

Claim 2 (Year 2): +25% additional increase
Year 2: $1,200 + ($1,000 × 0.25) = $1,450
Year 3: $1,400 + ($1,000 × 0.20) = $1,400
Year 4: $1,300 + ($1,000 × 0.15) = $1,450
Year 5: $1,000 × 1.05 = $1,050

WORSE IMPACT: Multiple claims affect you for longer AND can
prevent renewal (insurer may cancel) or force higher excess
```

## SECTION 6: Special Circumstances

### Factors That Increase Premium Impact

```
FACTORS INCREASING PREMIUM IMPACT
═══════════════════════════════════

High Impact Factors (multiply increase × 1.5-2.0):
☐ This is your 2nd or 3rd claim
☐ Claims within 2-3 years of each other
☐ Damage caused by your negligence
☐ Pattern of claims (multiple within 5 years)
☐ Large claim amount (>50% of sum insured)
☐ Fraud or exaggeration suspected

Moderate Impact Factors (multiply increase × 1.2-1.5):
☐ Second claim in 3-5 years
☐ You contributed to damage (partial negligence)
☐ First claim above $10,000
☐ High-risk property location

Lower Impact Factors (reduce increase × 0.5-0.8):
☐ First claim in 10+ years
☐ Completely not your fault (natural disaster)
☐ Claim below $5,000
☐ You have claims history elsewhere
```

### Factors That Decrease Premium Impact

```
FACTORS DECREASING PREMIUM IMPACT
═══════════════════════════════════

Improvements (may reduce impact):
☐ Better security system installed
☐ Property maintenance improved
☐ Moved to lower-risk area
☐ Claims prevention measures taken

Loyalty Discounts:
☐ Multiple policies with same insurer
☐ Long customer history (10+ years)
☐ No lapses in coverage
```

## SECTION 7: Insurer Conversation Guide

### Questions to Ask Your Insurer BEFORE Claiming

```
Call your insurer and ask:

"If I make a claim for approximately $[AMOUNT],
approximately how much might my premium increase?"

Insurer should be able to give you:
• Estimated premium increase percentage
• Duration of the increase
• Any factors affecting the calculation

Expected response: "Typically 10-25% depending on circumstances"

Record their answer:
Estimated increase: __________%
Duration: __________years
Conditions affecting rate: _________________________________________________________________

Note: Insurer won't give exact figure until claim processed,
but can give reasonable estimate.
```

### What to Say

```
CONVERSATION SCRIPT
═══════════════════

You: "Hello, I have a potential claim. Before I lodge it,
      I'd like to understand the premium impact. Can you help me?"

Insurer: [Will transfer to claims or customer service]

You: "I have damage estimated at approximately $[AMOUNT].
      This would be my [first/second] claim in [X] years.
      Approximately how much would my premium increase?"

Insurer: [Should give range, e.g., "typically 10-20%"]

You: "How long does that premium increase typically last?"

Insurer: [Should answer "usually 3-5 years, depending on claim history"]

You: "Thank you. I'll consider that and decide whether to claim."
```

## SECTION 8: Non-Financial Reasons to Claim (Or Not)

### Sometimes Decision is More Than Just Money

#### Reasons to Claim Despite Financial Loss

```
Non-Financial Reasons to Claim:
───────────────────────────────

☐ Peace of mind (claim covers you for future issues)
☐ Proper restoration (insurer pays professional, not DIY)
☐ Medical evidence (damage caused health issues)
☐ Legal liability (third party seeking compensation)
☐ Professional requirements (business needs restoration certified)
☐ Mortgage requirement (lender may require claim submission)
☐ Rental market (can't rent unrepaired property)
☐ Emotional impact (damage affected wellbeing significantly)
```

#### Reasons NOT to Claim Despite Financial Gain

```
Non-Financial Reasons NOT to Claim:
───────────────────────────────────

☐ Avoid stress of claim process
☐ Preserve claim history (keep as backup for future)
☐ Avoid insurer scrutiny of property/contents
☐ Don't want personal/financial information reviewed
☐ Prefer privacy (don't want insurer knowing details)
☐ Reputation concerns (some areas have high claims = stigma)
☐ Time cost (claim process takes 4 months)
☐ Preferred relationship with insurer (don't want friction)
```

## SECTION 9: Decision Framework

### Final Decision Tree

```
START HERE: Is damage significant? (>$500)
├─ NO → DON'T CLAIM (self-insure, pay from savings)
│
└─ YES → Is your excess covered by claim amount?
   ├─ NO → DON'T CLAIM (excess = full loss)
   │
   └─ YES → Is this your first claim in 3+ years?
      ├─ NO → Calculate break-even (Section 1)
      │   ├─ Break-even POSITIVE → CLAIM
      │   └─ Break-even NEGATIVE → DON'T CLAIM
      │
      └─ YES → Is damage your fault?
         ├─ NO → CLAIM (lowest premium impact)
         │
         └─ YES → Calculate break-even (Section 1)
             ├─ Break-even >$3,000 savings → CLAIM
             └─ Break-even <$3,000 savings → MAYBE CLAIM (consider non-financial factors)
```

## Useful Premium Estimates by Situation

### Average Premium Impact by Claim Type (Australia)

| Claim Type | Amount | Typical Premium Increase | Example: $1,200 Annual Premium Becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small - Theft | $500-$1,000 | 15-20% | $1,380-$1,440 |
| Small - Water | $1,000-$2,000 | 15-20% | $1,380-$1,440 |
| Small - Storm | $1,500-$2,500 | 10-15% | $1,320-$1,380 |
| Medium - Theft | $2,500-$5,000 | 20-25% | $1,440-$1,500 |
| Medium - Water | $3,000-$6,000 | 15-20% | $1,380-$1,440 |
| Large - Fire | $10,000-$25,000 | 10-15% | $1,320-$1,380 |
| Large - Structural | $25,000-$50,000 | 10-15% | $1,320-$1,380 |

**Notes**:
- First claim has lower impact than subsequent claims
- "At-fault" claims (your negligence) have higher impact
- Vary by insurer (shop around after claim)
- Premium returns to normal after 4-5 years without further claims
