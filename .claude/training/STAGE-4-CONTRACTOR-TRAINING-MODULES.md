## Training Program Overview

### Purpose
Equip NRPG contractors with professional knowledge to:
- Understand insurance policies and coverage
- Communicate effectively with clients and insurers
- Document damage professionally per insurer requirements
- Navigate three-way communication (client, contractor, insurer)
- Verify and maintain proper insurance credentials

### Target Audience
- NRPG-approved contractors (restoration, water damage, fire, mould specialists)
- New contractors before first insurance-backed job
- Existing contractors (annual refresher)
- Contractor staff and subcontractors

### Mandatory Completion
- **New contractors**: Must complete all 4 modules before first insurance-backed job
- **Existing contractors**: Annual refresher (1-2 modules per quarter)
- **Tracking**: Recorded in contractor profile with completion dates
- **Non-compliance**: Cannot accept insurance-backed jobs without current certification

## Training Curriculum Details

### Module 1: Insurance Policy Recognition & Communication (45 min)

#### Section 1: Understanding Policy Types (10 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Introduction (1 min)
   - "Why contractors need to understand policies"
   - Real scenario: Client shows policy, doesn't know what it covers

2. Building Insurance Overview (4 min)
   - Definition: Structure + permanent fixtures
   - What's covered: Fire, storm, burst pipes, etc.
   - What's NOT covered: Wear/tear, maintenance, betterment
   - Common confusion: "My roof isn't covered" (usually IS covered under storm)

3. Contents Insurance Overview (3 min)
   - Definition: Personal belongings inside
   - What's covered: Furniture, appliances, clothing, electronics
   - Temporary accommodation section
   - Common confusion: Garage contents, items in storage

4. Commercial/Business Insurance (2 min)
   - Differences from residential
   - Business interruption basics
   - Inventory coverage

**Interactive Elements**:
- Policy document screenshots (show actual pages)
- Highlight feature: Clickable policy sections
- Scenario questions: "Is this covered?" (reveals answer)
- Knowledge check: 3 questions before moving to Section 2

#### Section 2: Identifying Coverage Gaps (8 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Mandatory vs Optional Coverage (3 min)
   - Mandatory: Fire, storm, burst pipes, malicious damage, impact
   - Optional: Flood, accidental damage, equipment breakdown
   - Why this matters: Affects what you can offer to fix

2. The Flood Cover Gap (2 min)
   - Most major insurers: Flood is optional
   - Allianz exception: Included
   - Client impact: Can't claim flood damage without cover
   - Contractor impact: You can't repair without approval

3. Other Common Gaps (2 min)
   - Accidental damage (not standard)
   - Enhanced temporary accommodation (limits apply)
   - Business interruption (separate policy needed)
   - Betterment (upgrade costs not covered)

4. Asking the Right Questions (1 min)
   - "Does your policy include flood?" (Critical)
   - "Do you have accidental damage cover?"
   - "What's your excess?" (Affects negotiation)

**Interactive Elements**:
- "Find the gap" scenario (client situation, identify what's not covered)
- Decision tree: "Is this covered?" (guides through logic)
- Callout boxes: "Contractor talking point" (how to discuss with client)

#### Section 3: Professional Communication About Policy (7 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Do's & Don'ts of Policy Communication (2 min)
   - DO: "Your policy covers fire damage"
   - DON'T: "Your insurer won't pay for that"
   - DO: "I recommend checking with your insurer"
   - DON'T: Give specific insurance advice
   - Reason: You're not an insurance advisor

2. Explaining Exclusions Professionally (2 min)
   - Client says: "But I'm insured!"
   - You say: "Let's check what your policy covers"
   - Show the policy section (don't guess)
   - Be empathetic: "I know that's frustrating"

3. Boundary Setting (2 min)
   - What you CAN do: Explain policy language
   - What you CAN'T do: Tell them they're not covered
   - Direct them: "Contact your insurer for coverage questions"
   - Escalate when needed: "This is a coverage dispute"

4. Building Trust Through Honesty (1 min)
   - "I'm not an insurance expert, but based on your policy..."
   - "Let me get clarification from your insurer"
   - "Your insurance agent can answer that definitively"

**Interactive Elements**:
- Video scenarios: "How would you respond?" (reveals best response)
- Role-play dialogue: Client question → Professional response
- Tone analysis: "Empathetic" vs "dismissive" (audio examples)
- Reflection question: Why boundary-setting matters

### Module 2: Documentation Standards for Insurance Work (30 min)

#### Section 1: Professional Photo Documentation (8 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Why Photos Matter (1 min)
   - Determines claim approval speed (30-40% faster with good photos)
   - Prevents disputes (visual evidence is clear)
   - Reduces back-and-forth (one complete set is better than multiple batches)
   - Real impact: One client's claim approved in 2 weeks vs 8 weeks (only difference: photos)

2. Insurer-Specific Requirements (3 min)
   - NRMA: 2+ photos per area (minimum)
   - Suncorp: 3+ photos per area (strict requirement)
   - Allianz: 2-3 photos per area (flexible)
   - QBE: 2+ photos per area
   - General rule: Wide shot + close-ups (2-3 angles)
   - Video shows examples for each insurer

3. Technical Photography Tips (3 min)
   - Lighting: Natural light is best (avoid dark interiors)
   - Camera: Phone camera sufficient (high resolution)
   - Framing: Include context (show before/after areas)
   - Distance: Close-ups to show damage clearly
   - Date stamp: Use phone's automatic feature
   - Avoid: Blurry, dark, or confusing angles

4. What to Photograph (1 min)
   - Full room overview (wide angle from doorway)
   - Damage detail (close-ups, multiple angles)
   - Waterlines, scorch marks, collapsed areas
   - Undamaged areas (for comparison)
   - Extent of spread (where damage ends)

**Interactive Elements**:
- Before/after photo comparison (swipe slider)
- "Rate this photo" (good/bad examples)
- Interactive checklist: "What would you photograph?" (reveals all areas)
- Photo gallery: Real damage examples with annotations

#### Section 2: Quote Formatting for Different Insurers (6 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Quote Structure Insurers Expect (2 min)
   - Itemized breakdown (labor + materials separated)
   - Materials: Description + cost per unit
   - Labor: Hours × hourly rate
   - Subtotal before taxes
   - GST if applicable
   - Total amount
   - Quote validity (30 days minimum)
   - Contractor details (name, license, contact)

2. Insurer-Specific Preferences (2 min)
   - NRMA: Standard format, simple breakdown
   - Suncorp: Detailed materials list (wants to know what's included)
   - Allianz: Any professional format acceptable
   - QBE: Detailed breakdown for large items
   - General: More detail = faster approval

3. What NOT to Include (1 min)
   - Vague labor: "Labor - $2,000" (no - break down)
   - Assumptions: "Assume 2 coats paint" (ask first)
   - Upgrades: Don't include premium options (insurer won't pay)
   - Contingencies: No "if needed" items

4. Quote Examples by Damage Type (1 min)
   - Water damage: Labor (extraction, drying) + materials (dehumidifier rental)
   - Fire damage: Labor + materials (cleaning, paint, replacement)
   - Mould: Labor + materials (remediation, prevention)
   - Video shows annotated examples for each

**Interactive Elements**:
- Quote template: Editable sample (shows before/after)
- Common mistakes: "What's wrong with this quote?" (reveals issues)
- Comparison view: Good quote vs poor quote (side-by-side)
- Insurer preferences quiz: "Which format for [insurer]?" (reveals answer)

#### Section 3: Scope of Work Specifications (4 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. What is Scope of Work? (1 min)
   - Document describing exactly what you'll do
   - Why it matters: Prevents disputes, sets expectations, clarifies boundaries
   - Sent to insurer: Before work begins
   - Format: Simple, clear, detailed

2. Key Scope Elements (2 min)
   - What's included: "Remove water-damaged carpet, dispose, prepare for replacement"
   - What's NOT included: "Carpet replacement (client responsibility)"
   - Timeline: "5 days to remove water damage, prepare for flooring"
   - Costs: Reference to quote
   - Conditions: "Work cannot begin until insurance approval received"
   - Changes: "Any additions require written approval"

3. Examples by Damage Type (1 min)
   - Water damage scope: Extraction → Drying → Preparation
   - Fire damage scope: Cleaning → Repairs → Painting
   - Mould scope: Inspection → Remediation → Prevention
   - Each with clear start/end points

**Interactive Elements**:
- Scope template: Download-able for contractors
- Scenario: "Write the scope" (reveals model answer)
- Dispute prevention: "How this prevents problems" (shows consequences)

### Module 3: Three-Way Communication Mastery (60 min)

#### Section 1: Managing Client Expectations (10 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Why Expectations Matter (2 min)
   - Unhappy clients blame contractors (even if insurer refuses)
   - Unrealistic timelines cause stress
   - Misaligned expectations = disputes
   - Real scenario: Client expected 1 week, took 6 weeks (insurer assessment)

2. Setting Expectations Early (3 min)
   - At first contact: "Here's how this works"
   - Timeline conversation: "Typical timeline is 4-8 weeks"
   - Breakdown: Assessment (2-4 weeks) + Approval (2-4 weeks) + Work (1-2 weeks)
   - Variables: Damage complexity, insurer responsiveness
   - Documentation: Send email summarizing expectations

3. Interim Communication (3 min)
   - Weekly updates minimum (Code of Practice requires every 20 days)
   - Update format: "Waiting for insurer response" vs "We're ready to start"
   - Transparency: What you know, what you're waiting on
   - Honesty: "This is taking longer than expected because..."
   - Professionalism: "Here's what we're doing in the meantime"

4. Handling Disappointment (2 min)
   - When insurer rejects or reduces claim: "I understand you're disappointed"
   - Empathy + facts: "This is frustrating, but here's why the insurer said no"
   - Options: "Here are your next steps"
   - Escalation: "If you believe this is unfair, you can escalate to AFCA"

**Interactive Elements**:
- Email template: Sample update email (editable)
- Scenario roleplay: Client says "Why is this taking so long?" (reveals response)
- Timeline visualization: Interactive Gantt chart showing stages
- Empathy guide: "Do's and Don'ts" of tough conversations

#### Section 2: Communicating with Insurance Adjusters (10 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. Who Are You Talking To? (2 min)
   - Insurance adjuster: Assesses claim, makes coverage decisions
   - Claims assessor: Determines scope of work and costs
   - Authorizer: Approves payment (might be same person)
   - Your relationship: Professional, factual, solution-focused
   - Tone: Respectful but confident

2. What Adjusters Need From You (4 min)
   - Professional photos (per their requirements)
   - Clear quotes (itemized, realistic)
   - Honest scope of work (no inflated costs)
   - Timely responses (same-day if possible)
   - Professional communication (written is better than phone)
   - Problem-solving: "Here's the issue + my recommendation"

3. How to Present Information (3 min)
   - Email format: "Claim reference + subject line + clear message"
   - Document organization: Numbered items, clear references
   - Photo labeling: "Room name - damage type - angle" (not IMG_001.jpg)
   - Quote presentation: "As discussed, here's the detailed breakdown..."
   - Follow-up: "Please confirm receipt + timeline for response"

4. Building Credibility (1 min)
   - Consistency: Same message to adjuster and client
   - Accuracy: Facts backed by evidence (photos, quotes)
   - Professionalism: No complaints, no gossip, no shortcuts
   - Reliability: Respond when you say you will

**Interactive Elements**:
- Email templates: Ready-to-use for common scenarios
- Communication flowchart: "What should I send when?"
- Adjuster perspective: Video from adjuster (why they need certain info)
- Real email examples: Good vs poor communication (annotations)

#### Section 3: Handling Coverage Disputes Professionally (8 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. What is a Coverage Dispute? (2 min)
   - Disagreement about what's covered
   - Example: Insurer says "flood exclusion" / Client says "this is storm surge"
   - Example: Insurer says "wear and tear" / Client says "weather damage"
   - Contractor's role: Support client with facts, not opinions

2. Your Role As Contractor (2 min)
   - Document the damage (photos show what happened)
   - Provide facts (this is water damage, here's how it occurred)
   - Stay neutral: "Let's see what the insurer says"
   - Don't claim expertise: "This looks like storm damage to me, but the insurer will decide"
   - Escalate appropriately: "This disagreement needs the insurer/adjuster to resolve"

3. When Clients Disagree With Insurer (2 min)
   - Sympathize: "I understand you're frustrated"
   - Explain: "The insurer has assessed this as [coverage issue]"
   - Options: "You can request a reassessment or escalate to AFCA"
   - Documentation: "Here are the photos that show what happened"
   - Boundary: "I support you, but the coverage decision is the insurer's"

4. When You See a Coverage Error (2 min)
   - Flag it professionally: "The policy states [coverage], this appears to qualify"
   - Suggest reassessment: "I'd recommend asking the insurer to review"
   - Document your observation: Email to both client and insurer
   - Escalate if legitimate: "If this is incorrect, AFCA can review"
   - Protect yourself: Keep records of your communication

**Interactive Elements**:
- Scenario cases: 3 common disputes (reveals professional handling)
- Roleplay: Client angry about rejection (shows empathetic but factual response)
- Boundary guide: "What I can/cannot do"
- De-escalation techniques: Interactive dialogue

#### Section 4: Providing Status Updates (7 minutes video)

**Video Content**:
1. When & How to Update (2 min)
   - Frequency: Weekly minimum, every 20 days required by Code of Practice
   - Format: Email is professional, phone is acceptable, text is minimum
   - Content: What's happened, what's next, when they'll hear again
   - Proactive: Don't wait for them to ask

2. Update Templates (2 min)
   - Waiting on insurer: "Submitted documentation to [Insurer] on [date], expecting response by [date]"
   - Insurer approved: "Great news! [Insurer] approved scope of work for $[amount], work begins [date]"
   - Work in progress: "Work is [% complete], [specific update], on schedule for completion [date]"
   - Issues: "We encountered [issue], here's how we're addressing it"

3. Bad Update vs Good Update (2 min)
   - Bad: "Still waiting" (vague, unhelpful)
   - Good: "We submitted all documentation to Suncorp on March 5. Their standard assessment timeline is 2-4 weeks. We should have a response by March 19. I'll update you as soon as I hear back."

   - Bad: "Problem with the roof" (unclear)
   - Good: "The hidden water damage to the roof joists was more extensive than initial assessment. I've sent revised photos and scope to [Insurer]. This may increase the claim by approximately $2,000, pending their approval."

4. Difficult Updates (1 min)
   - Bad news: "The insurer won't cover [item] because of [exclusion]. Here's what the policy says... Here are your options"
   - Delays: "The assessment is taking longer than expected because [reason]. Here's what I'm doing to move it forward"
   - Changes: "The scope has changed from [original] to [new] because [reason]. New estimate is [amount]"

**Interactive Elements**:
- Email template library: 10+ ready-to-use update templates
- "Good vs bad update" comparison (side-by-side)
- Update checklist: "Did I include everything?" (reveals requirements)
- Tone analyzer: Does this sound professional? (gives feedback)

### Module 4: Contractor Insurance Requirements for NRPG (25 min)

#### Section 1: Public Liability Insurance - $10M Standard (6 minutes)

**Video Content**:
1. What is Public Liability Insurance? (1 min)
   - Covers: Bodily injury or property damage YOU cause to third parties
   - Example: Your team damages client's fence during work
   - Example: Client's neighbor injured on the property
   - NOT covered: Your own equipment damage, employee injuries (that's WorkCover)

2. Why $10M For Disaster Work? (2 min)
   - Industry standard for restoration/reconstruction
   - Covers: Large-scale property damage claims
   - Risk exposure: Potential damage in restoration work is substantial
   - Insurer requirement: NRPG contractors typically required for all insurance work
   - Cost: Usually $1,000-$2,000 annually for contractors

3. Certificate of Currency (2 min)
   - What it is: Proof your insurance is active
   - Shows: Policy number, expiry date, coverage limit, insurer
   - When needed: Before starting insurance-backed jobs

#### Section 2: Workers Compensation by State (5 minutes)

2. State-by-State Breakdown (3 min)

   - QLD: WorkSafe Queensland - compulsory
   - SA: ReturnToWorkSA - compulsory

   - ACT: ACT Workcover - compulsory

   - Other states: Not yet mandatory, but increasingly required by clients
