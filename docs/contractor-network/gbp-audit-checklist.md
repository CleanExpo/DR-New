# Quarterly Contractor GBP Audit Checklist

**Purpose:** NRPG's Ask Maps visibility depends on the quality of contractor Google Business Profile (GBP) listings across the network. Quarterly audits catch degraded profiles before they hurt network-wide ranking. Each contractor's GBP health directly contributes to the network's collective local authority.

**Frequency:** Quarterly — audit 10 contractors per cycle, rotating through the full network.

---

## 1. Audit Purpose and Frequency

### Why this audit matters

Google's local ranking algorithm factors in the completeness, activity, and consistency of individual GBP profiles. For a networked service model like NRPG:
- A weak contractor profile can suppress network-wide visibility in that contractor's service area.
- Inconsistent NAP data across contractor profiles reduces Google's confidence in the network as a whole.
- Low review response rates signal poor engagement and can depress individual profile rankings.

### Audit cadence

| Cycle | Timing | Contractors audited |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | First week of January | Contractors 1–10 (alphabetical or by region) |
| Q2 | First week of April | Contractors 11–20 |
| Q3 | First week of July | Contractors 21–30 |
| Q4 | First week of October | Contractors 31–40 (or cycle back to 1–10 if network < 40) |

- Each contractor is audited at least once per year (more frequently if flagged as underperforming).
- Newly onboarded contractors should receive an initial GBP audit within 30 days of onboarding, regardless of the quarterly cycle.

## 4. Remediation Process

For contractors who score below standard (Fair or Poor):

### Step 1: Generate contractor-specific fix list
- From the checklist above, extract all "Fail" items.
- Group by priority: Identity/Branding issues (Section A) are highest priority; Activity issues (Section D) are medium priority.

### Step 2: Send optimisation support communication
- Use the standard NRPG contractor optimisation support template.
- Reference: DR-459 (contractor GBP support process) for the full support workflow and communication templates.
- Include the specific Fail items for that contractor — do not send a generic "improve your profile" message.

### Step 3: Set a follow-up date
- Standard follow-up: 4 weeks after support communication is sent.
- Expedited follow-up (for Priority Fixes on A1, A2, B1, B2): 2 weeks.

### Step 4: Re-audit at follow-up
- Re-run only the failed checks from the original audit.
- Update the audit log with the re-audit date and revised pass count.
- If the contractor has not acted on Priority Fixes within 4 weeks of the support communication, escalate to the account manager.

### Priority fix definitions

| Priority | Items | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | A1 (business name), A2 (NRPG language), B1 (phone), B2 (website) | Must be corrected within 14 days |
| High | A3 (IICRC), A4 (photo), B3 (service area), B4 (hours), C1 (primary category) | Must be corrected within 30 days |
| Standard | C2 (secondary categories), C3 (services listed), D1 (posts), D2 (review responses) | Must be corrected within 60 days |

## 5. Network-Wide GBP Health Score

After each quarterly audit cycle, calculate a network health score to track improvement over time.

### Calculation method

1. **Collect individual audit scores** for all contractors audited in the cycle.
   - Each contractor has a score out of 13 (total checks).

2. **Calculate average pass rate:**

   ```
   Network Health Score = (Sum of all "Checks Passed" across all contractors audited) / (Number of contractors audited × 13) × 100
   ```

   Example: 10 contractors audited, total 108 checks passed out of 130 possible.
   `108 / 130 × 100 = 83.1%`

3. **Categorise the network score:**

| Score | Rating | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100% | Excellent | Maintain current standards; note best-practice contractors |
| 75–89% | Good | Minor improvements needed; continue quarterly cadence |
| 60–74% | Fair | Increase audit frequency to bi-monthly; run network-wide optimisation campaign |
| Below 60% | Poor | Urgent: run immediate network-wide remediation; escalate to leadership |
