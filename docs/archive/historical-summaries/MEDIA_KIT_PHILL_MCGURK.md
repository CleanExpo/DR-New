## SAMPLE Q&A FOR JOURNALISTS

### Q: What makes you qualified to comment on Brisbane disaster recovery trends?
**A:** "As one of Queensland's limited IICRC Master Restorers - the highest certification in the restoration industry - I respond to 500+ emergency projects annually across Brisbane, Ipswich, and Logan. I see firsthand what's happening in the market: response times, costs, insurance trends, and property damage patterns. This data comes directly from real projects, not estimates or surveys."

### Q: Why have disaster recovery costs increased so much?
**A:** "Three main factors: Brisbane's building stock is aging and more vulnerable to severe damage; climate change is creating more intense weather events with deeper water penetration; and the restoration industry hasn't expanded capacity to match demand, leading to longer response times and more secondary damage. What used to be a straightforward water extraction job now requires extensive structural drying and mould remediation."

### Q: What should Brisbane property owners do to prepare for flood season?
**A:** "Three critical steps: First, know your property's flood history - check Brisbane City Council flood maps and ask previous owners. Second, have an emergency response plan including who to call 24/7, where your water shut-offs are, and evacuation protocols. Third, document your property now with photos and videos for insurance purposes. The time to prepare is before the flood, not during."

### Q: How can people avoid buying a property with hidden water damage?
**A:** "Standard building inspections often miss water damage because they're visual assessments. For properties in flood-prone suburbs like Hamilton, Ascot, or New Farm - especially Queenslanders - insist on moisture mapping and subfloor inspection using thermal imaging and moisture meters. It costs $500-800 extra but can save you $100,000+ in undisclosed damage."

### Q: What's the biggest mistake property owners make during disasters?
**A:** "Waiting. The most common call I get is 'We had water come through three days ago, and now there's a smell.' By then, mould is growing, structures are weakening, and costs have doubled. The first 24 hours are critical. Even if you're waiting for insurance approval, at minimum you need to stop the water source, extract standing water, and start drying. Every hour of delay adds damage."
