# Emergency Water Damage Response - FAQ Guide

## Immediate Response (First 30 Minutes)

### Critical Safety Steps
**Q: What should I do first when I discover water damage?**
A: Your safety is paramount. Follow these steps:
1. Turn off electricity to affected areas (if safe to do so)
2. Stop the water source if possible (turn off main valve)
3. Call emergency services if there's risk of structural damage
4. Contact professional water damage restoration (1300 309 361)

### Documentation for Insurance
**Q: How should I document water damage for insurance claims?**
A: Proper documentation is crucial:
- Take photos/videos from multiple angles
- Document the water source and extent of damage
- List affected items and their approximate value
- Keep receipts for any immediate expenses
- Don't throw away damaged items until adjuster approval

## First 24 Hours Response

### Professional Assessment
**Q: How quickly should professionals assess water damage?**
A: Within 24 hours is critical because:
- Mold growth can begin within 24-48 hours
- Secondary damage increases exponentially
- Insurance coverage may require prompt action
- Structural integrity can deteriorate rapidly

### What to Expect from Professionals
**Q: What happens during a professional water damage assessment?**
A: Our certified technicians will:
1. Conduct moisture readings with specialized equipment
2. Assess the category and class of water damage
3. Develop a comprehensive restoration plan
4. Document everything for insurance purposes
5. Begin immediate water extraction if approved

## Water Damage Categories

### Category 1: Clean Water
**Q: What is Category 1 water damage?**
A: Clean water from sources like:
- Broken supply lines
- Overflowing sinks
- Appliance malfunctions
- Generally safe to handle with proper equipment

### Category 2: Gray Water
**Q: What constitutes gray water damage?**
A: Slightly contaminated water from:
- Washing machine overflow
- Dishwasher leaks
- Toilet overflow (urine only)
- Requires professional handling

### Category 3: Black Water
**Q: Why is black water dangerous?**
A: Highly contaminated water containing:
- Sewage backup
- Flood water from rivers/streams
- Toilet overflow with feces
- Requires specialized hazmat procedures

## Brisbane-Specific Considerations

### Flood Risk Areas
**Q: Which Brisbane areas are most at risk for flooding?**
A: High-risk areas include:
- River proximity zones (South Bank, New Farm)
- Low-lying areas (St Lucia, Toowong)
- Creek-adjacent suburbs (Breakfast Creek area)
- Historical flood zones (1974, 2011 flood maps)

### Seasonal Considerations
**Q: When is Brisbane most vulnerable to water damage?**
A: Peak risk periods:
- Storm season (October - April)
- La Niña weather patterns
- King tide events
- Severe weather warnings from Bureau of Meteorology

## Insurance Claim Process

### Immediate Steps
**Q: What should I do about insurance claims?**
A: Act quickly:
1. Contact your insurer within 24 hours
2. Provide initial damage assessment
3. Request emergency mitigation approval
4. Keep all receipts for emergency expenses
5. Work with preferred restoration contractors

### Working with Adjusters
**Q: How do restoration companies work with insurance adjusters?**
A: We coordinate directly:
- Provide detailed scope of work
- Use insurance industry software (Xactimate)
- Document all work with photos
- Communicate progress regularly
- Ensure compliance with policy requirements

## Prevention and Mitigation

### Home Maintenance
**Q: How can I prevent water damage in Brisbane's climate?**
A: Regular maintenance includes:
- Clean gutters before storm season
- Inspect roof annually
- Check plumbing connections
- Install flood barriers in high-risk areas
- Maintain proper drainage around property

### Early Warning Systems
**Q: What early warning systems should Brisbane residents use?**
A: Monitor these sources:
- Bureau of Meteorology alerts
- Brisbane City Council flood warnings
- Emergency Alert system
- Local radio stations during severe weather
